# BoozeTutor Wine Curriculum

The course is organized in strands. Each strand groups lessons, and each lesson teaches one or more topics; every topic says what you should be able to do once you’ve finished it.

Every topic has a level. Basic covers the essentials for enjoying and talking about a drink with confidence. Intermediate adds the grounding of a keen enthusiast or someone starting out in the trade. Advanced goes deeper, into the science, the finer points of production and regions in detail. You choose a level for each drink, and its Lessons list shows the topics up to that level.

## 1. Where wine comes from

### 1.1 The vine and its climate

The vine, the vineyard and why place matters

- **1.1.1 Welcome** (Basic): Know what the course covers and how to use it.
- **1.1.2 The vine and its growing season** (Basic): Explain what a vine needs and how it grows through the year.
- **1.1.3 Climate, weather and vintage** (Basic): Explain why climate, weather and place shape a wine’s style.
- **1.1.4 Vineyard management** (Basic): Describe how growers train, protect and farm their vines, from phylloxera to organic and sustainable farming.

## 2. Tasting

### 2.1 How to taste

Systematic evaluation framework & guided red wine tasting

- **2.1.1 How to taste** (Basic): Taste a wine systematically and compare two wines.

## 3. How wine is made

### 3.1 Styles of wine

Still, sparkling, sweet and fortified wines

- **3.1.1 Styles of wine** (Basic): Name the main styles of wine and how a grape becomes each.

### 3.2 Red winemaking

From harvest to barrel

- **3.2.1 Red winemaking** (Basic): Describe how red wine is made, from maceration and pressing to oak; and carbonic maceration.

### 3.3 Internationally important black grapes

Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot, Syrah/Shiraz & Pinot Noir

- **3.3.1 International black grapes** (Basic): Recognize Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot, Syrah and Pinot Noir and where they grow.

### 3.4 White and rosé winemaking

White styles, sur lie, and tasting

- **3.4.1 White, rosé and orange winemaking** (Basic): Describe how white, rosé and orange wines are made.

### 3.5 Internationally important white grapes

Chardonnay, Sauvignon Blanc, Pinot Grigio & Riesling

- **3.5.1 International white grapes** (Basic): Recognize Chardonnay, Sauvignon Blanc, Pinot Gris and Riesling and where they grow.

### 3.6 Sparkling wines

The traditional and tank methods, Champagne and Prosecco

- **3.6.1 Sparkling wines** (Basic): Explain the traditional and tank methods, the main regions and the label terms.

### 3.7 Sweet and fortified wines

Sweet wines, Port, Sherry and Madeira

- **3.7.1 Sweet wines** (Basic): Explain how sweet wines are made and how growers concentrate sugar.
- **3.7.2 Fortified wines** (Basic): Explain how Port, Sherry, Madeira and Vin Doux Naturel are made.

### 3.8 Wine chemistry

Acids, phenolics, aromas, aging and closures

- **3.8.1 What’s in wine** (Advanced): Explain wine’s acids, phenolics and aroma compounds, how they age, and how vessels, oxygen, sulfur and closures change them.

## 4. The wine world

### 4.1 The wine world and its labels

Old and New World, geographical indications and quality hierarchies

- **4.1.1 Old World and New World** (Basic): Contrast Old and New World wine, and say what a village name tells you.
- **4.1.2 Reading the label** (Basic): Read a wine label: geographical indications, EU classification, and national and regional hierarchies.

### 4.2 France

Regions and grapes, from Bordeaux to the Rhône

- **4.2.1 France** (Basic): Know France’s main regions and their grapes, from Cabernet Franc and Chenin to Grenache.

### 4.3 Italy

Regions and grapes, from Piedmont to the south

- **4.3.1 Italy** (Basic): Know Italy’s regions and its main red and white grapes.

### 4.4 Spain and Portugal

Regions, Tempranillo, Albariño and Spanish aging terms

- **4.4.1 Spain and Portugal** (Basic): Know the regions of Spain and Portugal, Tempranillo and Albariño, and Spain’s aging terms.

### 4.5 Germany, Austria and Hungary

Regions, German labeling and Furmint

- **4.5.1 Germany, Austria and Hungary** (Intermediate): Know the regions of Germany, Austria and Hungary, German labeling, and Furmint.

### 4.6 Terroir

Site, soil, rootstock, clone and vine age

- **4.6.1 Terroir** (Advanced): Explain how site, soil, rootstock, clone and vine age shape what is in the glass.

### 4.7 Classic regions in depth

Burgundy, Bordeaux, Champagne, Barolo, Rioja and the Mosel

- **4.7.1 Classic European regions in depth** (Advanced): Explain the village and vineyard hierarchies of the Côte d’Or, Bordeaux, Champagne, Barolo and Barbaresco, Rioja and the Mosel.

### 4.8 The Americas

USA, Argentina and Chile

- **4.8.1 The Americas** (Intermediate): Know the wine regions of the USA, Argentina and Chile, US labeling, and Zinfandel, Malbec and Carmenère.

### 4.9 Australia, New Zealand and South Africa

Regions, labeling and Pinotage

- **4.9.1 Australia, New Zealand and South Africa** (Intermediate): Know the regions and labeling of Australia, New Zealand and South Africa, and Pinotage.

## 5. Serving and judging wine

### 5.1 Service & hospitality

Glassware, decanting, cellar management, wine faults & food pairing

- **5.1.1 Serving wine** (Basic): Choose glassware, decant, serve at the right temperature, keep a cellar and serve responsibly.
- **5.1.2 Wine faults** (Basic): Recognize oxidation, closure, sulfur and microbial faults.
- **5.1.3 Judging quality** (Intermediate): Describe a wine and justify a quality and readiness judgment.
- **5.1.4 Food and wine** (Basic): Pair food and wine and explain why a pairing works.

### 5.2 Final review

Review quiz and course conclusion

- **5.2.1 Final review** (Basic): Review everything in the course.
