# BoozeTutor Sake curriculum

The course is organized in strands. Each strand groups lessons, and each lesson teaches one or more topics; every topic says what you should be able to do once you’ve finished it.

Every topic has a level. Basic covers the essentials, enough to talk about the subject with confidence. Intermediate adds the grounding of a keen enthusiast or someone starting out in the trade. Advanced goes deeper, into the science, the finer points of production, and regions in detail. You choose a level for each course, and its Lessons list shows the topics up to that level.

## 1. Rice and brewing

### 1.1 Introduction to sake

What is sake and why is it unique?

- **1.1.1 Welcome** (Basic): Find your way around the sake course and its three levels.
- **1.1.2 Oshaku: pouring for others** (Basic): Pour sake for others and accept a pour as Japanese custom expects.
- **1.1.3 What sake is** (Basic): Explain what sake is made from and how it differs from wine and beer.
- **1.1.4 Sake rice** (Basic): Distinguish sake rice from table rice and name the main sake rice varieties.
- **1.1.5 Growing sake rice** (Intermediate): Describe how sake rice is grown through the year and why its origin matters.
- **1.1.6 Reading a sake label** (Basic): Read the grade, polishing ratio and other key facts on a sake label.

### 1.2 How sake is made

The production process from rice to bottle

- **1.2.1 Polishing and preparing rice** (Basic): Explain why rice is polished, washed, soaked and steamed.
- **1.2.2 Kōji** (Basic): Explain how kōji turns rice starch into sugar.
- **1.2.3 Fermentation in brief** (Basic): Describe how the starter and the main mash turn rice into sake, and how temperature shapes its style.
- **1.2.4 The yeast starter** (Intermediate): Compare the kimoto, yamahai and sokujō yeast starters.
- **1.2.5 Main fermentation** (Intermediate): Describe the three-stage main fermentation and multiple parallel fermentation.
- **1.2.6 Pressing** (Intermediate): Compare the ways sake is pressed and what each does to it.
- **1.2.7 From press to bottle** (Basic): Describe the steps from press to bottle: filtering, pasteurizing, diluting and maturing.
- **1.2.8 What the brewer controls** (Basic): Explain the main choices a brewer makes and how they show in the glass.

### 1.3 Sake science

Kōji, rice, fermentation and the numbers

- **1.3.1 Kōji and its enzymes** (Advanced): Explain how brewers steer kōji’s enzymes to balance sugar and flavor.
- **1.3.2 Rice science** (Advanced): Explain why sake rice behaves differently from table rice in the brewery.
- **1.3.3 Fermentation control** (Advanced): Explain how brewers steer and stop fermentation by temperature.
- **1.3.4 Pressing and flavor** (Advanced): Explain how pressure, timing and temperature at the press change a sake’s flavor.
- **1.3.5 The numbers on the label** (Advanced): Interpret the nihonshu-do, acidity and amino-acid values on a label.
- **1.3.6 Kyōkai yeast strains** (Advanced): Name the main Kyōkai yeast strains and what each gives a sake.

## 2. Styles and tasting

### 2.1 Sake grades & styles

Understanding the classification system

- **2.1.1 The grades** (Basic): Name the sake grades and the polishing ratio each requires.
- **2.1.2 Added alcohol** (Basic): Explain why some sake has alcohol added and what it changes.
- **2.1.3 The grades at a glance** (Basic): Compare the sake grades side by side.
- **2.1.4 Special types of sake** (Basic): Recognize nama, nigori, sparkling and aged sake.
- **2.1.5 Ginjō-zukuri** (Intermediate): Explain how ginjō-style brewing creates fruity aromas.
- **2.1.6 Sake and aging** (Intermediate): Explain how sake changes in storage.
- **2.1.7 Hiyaoroshi and kōshū** (Intermediate): Describe hiyaoroshi and kōshū and how they are made.

### 2.2 Tasting & evaluation

Flavor types, sensory vocabulary, and tasting methodology

- **2.2.1 Sake flavor profiles** (Basic): Describe the main sake flavor profiles.
- **2.2.2 Ginjō and junmai: getting started** (Basic): Compare a ginjō and a junmai side by side.
- **2.2.3 Appearance** (Basic): Describe a sake’s clarity and color.
- **2.2.4 Aroma** (Basic): Describe a sake’s aromas and where they come from.
- **2.2.5 Flavor** (Basic): Describe a sake’s sweetness, acidity, umami and body.
- **2.2.6 Finish** (Basic): Describe a sake’s finish.
- **2.2.7 Assessing a sake** (Basic): Assess a sake’s quality and give reasons.
- **2.2.8 Kikizake** (Advanced): Describe the kikizake tasting method and how competitions judge sake.
- **2.2.9 Tasting kimoto and modern sake** (Intermediate): Compare a kimoto or yamahai sake with a modern one.
- **2.2.10 Tasting nama and kōshū** (Basic): Compare a nama with a kōshū.
- **2.2.11 Faults** (Advanced): Recognize hine-ka, nama-hine-ka and the other sake faults.

### 2.3 Beyond sake

Japanese liqueurs and modern styles

- **2.3.1 Beyond sake** (Intermediate): Name the drinks sold beside sake and what sets them apart.
- **2.3.2 Sparkling, aged and low-alcohol sake** (Intermediate): Describe how sparkling, aged and low-alcohol sakes are made.
- **2.3.3 Yuzushu** (Intermediate): Describe yuzushu and the Japanese liqueurs sold with sake.
- **2.3.4 Umeshu** (Intermediate): Describe umeshu and how it is made.

## 3. Sake in Japan

### 3.1 Sake history & people

Ancient origins, kuramoto and tōji, and the yeast strains behind modern sake

- **3.1.1 Ancient origins** (Intermediate): Trace sake from its ancient origins.
- **3.1.2 Kudarizake and Nada** (Intermediate): Explain kudarizake and how Nada rose to lead sake brewing.
- **3.1.3 The modern era** (Intermediate): Describe how sake brewing changed in the modern era.
- **3.1.4 Kuramoto and tōji** (Intermediate): Describe the roles of the kuramoto and the tōji.
- **3.1.5 The tōji guilds** (Advanced): Compare the tōji guilds and the brewing styles they carry.

### 3.2 Regions of Japan

Key sake-producing prefectures and their styles

- **3.2.1 Water** (Intermediate): Explain how hard and soft water shape a sake.
- **3.2.2 Nada and Fushimi** (Intermediate): Describe the sake of Nada and Fushimi.
- **3.2.3 Niigata and Yamagata** (Intermediate): Describe the sake of Niigata and Yamagata.
- **3.2.4 Akita, Hiroshima and Fukushima** (Intermediate): Describe the sake of Akita, Hiroshima and Fukushima.
- **3.2.5 The GI system** (Advanced): Explain Japan’s GI system and its sake GIs.

## 4. Serving sake

### 4.1 Serving & food pairing

How to serve, store, and pair sake

- **4.1.1 Serving temperature** (Basic): Serve sake chilled, at room temperature or warm, and warm it well.
- **4.1.2 The nine serving temperatures** (Advanced): Name the nine traditional serving temperatures.
- **4.1.3 Temperature by grade** (Basic): Choose a serving temperature for each grade.
- **4.1.4 Glassware and storage** (Basic): Choose a vessel for sake and store sake to keep it fresh.
- **4.1.5 Food pairing** (Basic): Pair sake with food.
- **4.1.6 Responsible service** (Basic): Serve sake responsibly.
- **4.1.7 The temperature experiment** (Basic): Compare one sake served at three temperatures.

### 4.2 Final review

Review quiz across all sake topics

- **4.2.1 Final review** (Basic): Combine what each lesson taught to answer questions from across the course.
