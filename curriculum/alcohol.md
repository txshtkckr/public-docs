# BoozeTutor Alcohol curriculum

The course is organised in strands. Each strand groups lessons, and each lesson teaches one or more topics; every topic says what you should be able to do once you’ve finished it.

Every topic has a level. Basic covers the essentials for enjoying and talking about a drink with confidence. Intermediate adds the grounding of a keen enthusiast or someone starting out in the trade. Advanced goes deeper, into the science, the finer points of production and regions in detail. You choose a level for each drink, and its Lessons list shows the topics up to that level.

## 1. How we perceive a drink

### 1.1 Sensory science fundamentals

How taste, smell, and flavour perception work across all beverages

- **1.1.1 Welcome** (Basic): Find your way around the Alcohol Science course and its three levels.
- **1.1.2 The five basic tastes** (Basic): Name the five basic tastes and where each comes from in a drink.
- **1.1.3 Smell** (Basic): Explain orthonasal and retronasal smell and why smell makes most of flavour.
- **1.1.4 Flavour wheels and thresholds** (Intermediate): Use a flavour wheel and explain sensory thresholds.
- **1.1.5 Mouthfeel and temperature** (Basic): Explain how mouthfeel, temperature and fatigue change what we taste.
- **1.1.6 Faults in every drink** (Intermediate): Recognise the faults found in every kind of drink.

## 2. The chemistry of alcohol

### 2.1 The chemistry of ethanol

Understanding the molecule at the heart of all alcoholic beverages

- **2.1.1 Atomic bonds** (Basic): Explain how atoms bond to form molecules.
- **2.1.2 Double bonds** (Basic): Explain double bonds and why they matter in flavour molecules.
- **2.1.3 Functional groups** (Basic): Recognise the main functional groups in the molecules of a drink.
- **2.1.4 Esters and beyond** (Advanced): Recognise esters and the functional groups beyond the basics.
- **2.1.5 The ethanol molecule** (Basic): Describe the ethanol molecule.
- **2.1.6 Polarity and hydrogen bonds** (Advanced): Explain polarity and the hydrogen bond network in ethanol and water.
- **2.1.7 Ethanol in water** (Advanced): Explain how ethanol behaves when mixed with water.
- **2.1.8 Fermentation** (Basic): Explain how yeast turns sugar into ethanol and carbon dioxide.
- **2.1.9 Secondary reactions** (Advanced): Explain the secondary reactions that make esters and other by-products.
- **2.1.10 Proof systems** (Basic): Convert between ABV and the proof systems.
- **2.1.11 pH, acidity and sulfites** (Intermediate): Explain pH, acidity and sulfites in drinks.

### 2.2 Types of alcohol

Comparing ethanol, methanol, isopropanol, and butanol

- **2.2.1 The four main alcohols** (Intermediate): Distinguish ethanol, methanol and the other common alcohols.
- **2.2.2 Methanol** (Intermediate): Explain why methanol is dangerous.
- **2.2.3 Heads, hearts and tails** (Intermediate): Explain heads, hearts and tails and why distillers make cuts.
- **2.2.4 Ethylene glycol and glycerol** (Intermediate): Distinguish ethylene glycol from glycerol and explain why one is toxic.

### 2.3 Denaturing

Why and how ethanol is made undrinkable for industrial use

- **2.3.1 Denatured alcohol** (Intermediate): Explain why and how ethanol is denatured.
- **2.3.2 Common denaturants** (Intermediate): Name the common denaturants and what each does.
- **2.3.3 Prohibition poisoning** (Intermediate): Describe how denatured alcohol poisoned drinkers during Prohibition.
- **2.3.4 Industrial ethanol** (Intermediate): Describe the industrial uses of ethanol.
- **2.3.5 Biofuel ethanol** (Intermediate): Explain how ethanol is made and used as a fuel.

## 3. Alcohol and the body

### 3.1 Metabolism & BAC

How the body processes alcohol from absorption to elimination

- **3.1.1 Absorption** (Basic): Explain how the body absorbs alcohol.
- **3.1.2 The ADH–ALDH pathway** (Basic): Describe how ADH and ALDH break alcohol down.
- **3.1.3 Blood alcohol concentration** (Basic): Explain what sets a person’s blood alcohol concentration.
- **3.1.4 Standard drinks** (Basic): Calculate standard drinks and compare how countries define them.
- **3.1.5 Genetic variation** (Advanced): Explain how genetic variants change alcohol metabolism.
- **3.1.6 Alcohol and medication** (Advanced): Explain how alcohol interacts with medication.
- **3.1.7 Pharmacogenetics** (Advanced): Compare alcohol metabolism variants across populations.
- **3.1.8 Measuring alcohol in the body** (Advanced): Explain how breath and blood tests measure alcohol.

### 3.2 Effects on the body

Short-term and long-term impacts of alcohol consumption

- **3.2.1 The nervous system** (Basic): Explain how alcohol depresses the central nervous system.
- **3.2.2 Liver, heart and calories** (Basic): Describe alcohol’s long-term effects on the liver and heart, and its calories.
- **3.2.3 Dehydration and hangovers** (Basic): Explain what causes dehydration and hangovers.
- **3.2.4 Sleep** (Basic): Explain how alcohol affects sleep.
- **3.2.5 Cancer risk** (Basic): Explain how alcohol raises the risk of cancer.
- **3.2.6 Pregnancy** (Basic): Explain the risks of alcohol in pregnancy.
- **3.2.7 Dependence and withdrawal** (Intermediate): Describe alcohol dependence and withdrawal.
- **3.2.8 Tolerance** (Advanced): Explain how tolerance to alcohol develops.
- **3.2.9 Reading the epidemiology** (Advanced): Interpret studies of alcohol and health.
- **3.2.10 Receptor pharmacology** (Advanced): Explain how alcohol acts on GABA and the brain’s other receptors.

### 3.3 Final review

Review quiz across all alcohol science topics

- **3.3.1 Final review** (Basic): Combine what each lesson taught to answer questions from across the course.
