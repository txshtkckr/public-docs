# BoozeTutor Wine Curriculum

The course is organised in strands. Each strand groups lessons, and each lesson teaches one or more topics; every topic says what you should be able to do once you’ve finished it.

Every topic has a level. Basic covers the essentials for enjoying and talking about a drink with confidence. Intermediate adds the grounding of a keen enthusiast or someone starting out in the trade. Advanced goes deeper, into the science, the finer points of production and regions in detail. You choose a level for each drink, and its Lessons list shows the topics up to that level.

## 1. Where wine comes from

### 1.1 The vine and its climate

The vine, the vineyard and why place matters

- **1.1.1 Welcome** (Basic): Know what the course covers and how to use it.
- **1.1.2 The vine and its growing season** (Basic): Explain what a vine needs and how it grows through the year.
- **1.1.3 Climate, weather and vintage** (Basic): Explain why climate, weather and place shape a wine’s style.
- **1.1.4 Phylloxera** (Basic): Explain what phylloxera did to the world’s vineyards and why most vines are grafted.
- **1.1.5 Vineyard management** (Intermediate): Describe the grower’s main decisions through the year, from pruning to harvest.
- **1.1.6 Training systems and the canopy** (Advanced): Compare training systems and explain how canopy management shapes ripening.
- **1.1.7 Vine diseases** (Advanced): Recognise the main fungal, viral and bacterial vine diseases and how growers fight them.
- **1.1.8 Organic, biodynamic and sustainable farming** (Advanced): Distinguish organic, biodynamic and sustainable viticulture and their certifications.

## 2. Tasting

### 2.1 How to taste

Systematic evaluation framework & guided red wine tasting

- **2.1.1 Getting started** (Basic): Taste two wines side by side and say how they differ.
- **2.1.2 Appearance** (Basic): Describe a wine’s clarity, intensity and colour.
- **2.1.3 Aroma** (Basic): Describe a wine’s aromas by intensity, character and where they come from.
- **2.1.4 Flavour and finish** (Basic): Describe sweetness, acidity, tannin, alcohol, body, flavour and finish.
- **2.1.5 Assessing a wine** (Basic): Reach a reasoned conclusion about a wine’s quality.

## 3. How wine is made

### 3.1 Styles of wine

Still, sparkling, sweet and fortified wines

- **3.1.1 Styles of wine** (Basic): Name the main styles of wine and how a grape becomes each.

### 3.2 Red winemaking

From harvest to barrel

- **3.2.1 The red winemaking process** (Basic): Describe how red wine is made: crushing, fermentation, maceration and pressing.
- **3.2.2 Carbonic maceration** (Intermediate): Explain carbonic maceration and the fruity style it gives.
- **3.2.3 Malolactic conversion, maturation and oak** (Intermediate): Explain what malolactic conversion, maturation and oak do to a red wine.

### 3.3 Internationally important black grapes

Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot, Syrah/Shiraz & Pinot Noir

- **3.3.1 Cabernet Sauvignon** (Basic): Recognise Cabernet Sauvignon and where it grows.
- **3.3.2 Merlot** (Basic): Recognise Merlot and where it grows.
- **3.3.3 The Bordeaux blend** (Basic): Explain how Cabernet Sauvignon and Merlot combine in Bordeaux, Left Bank and Right.
- **3.3.4 Syrah/Shiraz** (Basic): Recognise Syrah and Shiraz and how the two styles differ.
- **3.3.5 Pinot Noir** (Basic): Recognise Pinot Noir and how place changes it.
- **3.3.6 The black grapes compared** (Basic): Tell the four black grapes apart by their structure and flavours.

### 3.4 White and rosé winemaking

White styles, sur lie, and tasting

- **3.4.1 White winemaking from pressing to bottle** (Basic): Describe how a white wine is made, from pressing and settling to bottling.
- **3.4.2 Shaping a white wine** (Intermediate): Explain how fermentation, malolactic conversion, lees contact and fining shape a white wine’s style.
- **3.4.3 Rosé winemaking** (Basic): Describe the ways rosé is made.
- **3.4.4 Orange/amber wine** (Advanced): Explain how orange wine is made and how it tastes.

### 3.5 Internationally important white grapes

Chardonnay, Sauvignon Blanc, Pinot Grigio & Riesling

- **3.5.1 Chardonnay** (Basic): Recognise Chardonnay, oaked and unoaked, and where it grows.
- **3.5.2 Sauvignon Blanc** (Basic): Recognise Sauvignon Blanc and where it grows.
- **3.5.3 Pinot Gris/Grigio** (Basic): Recognise Pinot Grigio and Pinot Gris and the two styles from Italy and Alsace.
- **3.5.4 Riesling** (Basic): Recognise Riesling, dry to sweet, and where it grows.
- **3.5.5 The white grapes compared** (Basic): Place the four white grapes on the spectrum from light to full and tell them apart.

### 3.6 Sparkling wines

The traditional and tank methods, Champagne and Prosecco

- **3.6.1 The traditional method** (Basic): Explain how traditional-method sparkling wine is made and how it tastes.
- **3.6.2 Tank method, Asti and the regions** (Basic): Explain the tank method and name the main sparkling wine regions.
- **3.6.3 Champagne and sparkling label terms** (Intermediate): Read a sparkling wine label: sweetness levels, vintage and producer terms.
- **3.6.4 Reserve wines, dosage and lees ageing** (Advanced): Explain how reserve wines, dosage and lees ageing shape Champagne.
- **3.6.5 Prosecco and Franciacorta** (Advanced): Distinguish Prosecco DOCG from DOC, and explain Franciacorta.

### 3.7 Sweet and fortified wines

Sweet wines, Port, Sherry and Madeira

- **3.7.1 Sweet winemaking** (Basic): Explain the ways a wine is left sweet.
- **3.7.2 Concentrating grape sugars** (Intermediate): Explain noble rot, late harvest, drying and ice wine, and taste a sweet wine.
- **3.7.3 Fortified wine and Port** (Basic): Explain how fortification works and how Port is made.
- **3.7.4 Sherry** (Basic): Explain how the styles of Sherry are made.
- **3.7.5 Solera and flor** (Advanced): Explain how the solera and flor shape Sherry.
- **3.7.6 Madeira and Vin Doux Naturel** (Intermediate): Explain how Madeira and Vin Doux Naturel are made.
- **3.7.7 Madeira: estufagem and canteiro** (Advanced): Compare Madeira’s estufagem and canteiro ageing.

### 3.8 Wine chemistry

Acids, phenolics, aromas, ageing and closures

- **3.8.1 Acids** (Advanced): Explain wine’s acids and what each brings.
- **3.8.2 Phenolics and ageing** (Advanced): Explain phenolics and carotenoids and how they evolve in bottle.
- **3.8.3 Aroma compounds** (Advanced): Explain where a wine’s aroma compounds come from.
- **3.8.4 Steel, concrete and clay** (Advanced): Compare fermenting and ageing vessels beyond oak.
- **3.8.5 Oxygen, sulfur and closures** (Advanced): Explain how oxygen, sulfur regimes and closures change a wine.

## 4. The wine world

### 4.1 The wine world and its labels

Old and New World, geographical indications and quality hierarchies

- **4.1.1 Old World and New World** (Basic): Contrast Old and New World wine, and say what a village name tells you.
- **4.1.2 Reading a label** (Basic): Read the basic information on a wine label and say why labelling matters.
- **4.1.3 Geographical indications** (Intermediate): Explain geographical indications and the EU’s classification of wine.
- **4.1.4 Quality hierarchies** (Intermediate): Explain national and regional quality hierarchies.

### 4.2 France

Regions and grapes, from Bordeaux to the Rhône

- **4.2.1 Bordeaux and Burgundy** (Basic): Know Bordeaux and Burgundy, their grapes and styles.
- **4.2.2 Champagne, Alsace, the Rhône and the Loire** (Intermediate): Know France’s other classic regions and their styles.
- **4.2.3 Cabernet Franc, Gamay and Grenache** (Intermediate): Recognise Cabernet Franc, Gamay and Grenache and where they grow.
- **4.2.4 Chenin Blanc, Viognier, Gewürztraminer and Semillon** (Intermediate): Recognise Chenin Blanc, Viognier, Gewürztraminer and Semillon and where they grow.

### 4.3 Italy

Regions and grapes, from Piedmont to the south

- **4.3.1 Italy’s wine regions** (Basic): Know Italy’s main wine regions and what they make.
- **4.3.2 Sangiovese** (Basic): Recognise Sangiovese and the wines it makes in Tuscany.
- **4.3.3 Nebbiolo, Barbera, Corvina and Montepulciano** (Intermediate): Recognise Italy’s other main red grapes and compare them.
- **4.3.4 Garganega, Fiano, Verdicchio and Cortese** (Intermediate): Recognise Italy’s main white grapes and compare them.

### 4.4 Spain and Portugal

Regions, Tempranillo, Albariño and Spanish ageing terms

- **4.4.1 Spain** (Intermediate): Know Spain’s main wine regions and what they make.
- **4.4.2 Tempranillo** (Basic): Recognise Tempranillo and where it grows.
- **4.4.3 Spanish ageing terms** (Intermediate): Explain Crianza, Reserva and Gran Reserva.
- **4.4.4 Albariño** (Intermediate): Recognise Albariño and where it grows.
- **4.4.5 Portugal** (Advanced): Know Portugal’s main table wine regions and grapes.

### 4.5 Germany, Austria and Hungary

Regions, German labelling and Furmint

- **4.5.1 Germany and its labels** (Intermediate): Know Germany’s regions and read a German wine label.
- **4.5.2 Austria** (Advanced): Know Austria’s main regions and grapes.
- **4.5.3 Furmint and Tokaji** (Intermediate): Recognise Furmint and the regional white grapes of central Europe.

### 4.6 Terroir

Site, soil, rootstock, clone and vine age

- **4.6.1 Vine energy and climate scale** (Advanced): Explain how a vine turns sunlight into sugar, and climate at macro, meso and micro scale.
- **4.6.2 Soils** (Advanced): Explain how soil drainage, heat and chemistry, and soil types, shape a wine, with classic examples.
- **4.6.3 Aspect, altitude and site climate** (Advanced): Explain how aspect, altitude, diurnal range and water stress show in the glass.
- **4.6.4 Rootstock, clone and vine age** (Advanced): Explain how rootstock, clone and vine age shape a wine.

### 4.7 Classic regions in depth

Burgundy, Bordeaux, Champagne, Barolo, Rioja and the Mosel

- **4.7.1 Burgundy: the Côte d’Or** (Advanced): Explain the Côte d’Or’s villages and vineyard classifications.
- **4.7.2 Bordeaux commune by commune** (Advanced): Explain Bordeaux’s communes and classifications.
- **4.7.3 Champagne: villages and crus** (Advanced): Explain Champagne’s villages and cru classification.
- **4.7.4 Barolo and Barbaresco** (Advanced): Explain Barolo’s and Barbaresco’s villages and vineyard names.
- **4.7.5 Rioja: zones, villages and age** (Advanced): Explain Rioja’s zones, village wines and ageing categories.
- **4.7.6 The Mosel, the Rheingau and the VDP** (Advanced): Explain single-vineyard naming and the VDP classification.

### 4.8 The Americas

USA, Argentina and Chile

- **4.8.1 The United States and its labels** (Intermediate): Know the main US wine regions and US labelling rules.
- **4.8.2 Zinfandel/Primitivo** (Intermediate): Recognise Zinfandel and Primitivo and where they grow.
- **4.8.3 Argentina and Malbec** (Intermediate): Know Argentina’s regions and recognise Malbec.
- **4.8.4 Chile and Carmenère** (Intermediate): Know Chile’s regions and recognise Carmenère.

### 4.9 Australia, New Zealand and South Africa

Regions, labelling and Pinotage

- **4.9.1 Australia** (Intermediate): Know Australia’s regions and its GI labelling.
- **4.9.2 New Zealand** (Intermediate): Know New Zealand’s regions and labelling.
- **4.9.3 South Africa and Pinotage** (Intermediate): Know South Africa’s regions and Wine of Origin scheme, and recognise Pinotage.

## 5. Serving and judging wine

### 5.1 Service & hospitality

Glassware, decanting, cellar management, wine faults & food pairing

- **5.1.1 Glassware and serving temperature** (Basic): Choose the right glass and serve each style at the right temperature.
- **5.1.2 Decanting** (Basic): Say when and how to decant a wine.
- **5.1.3 Cellar management** (Intermediate): Store wine well and manage a cellar.
- **5.1.4 Responsible service** (Basic): Serve wine responsibly.
- **5.1.5 Oxidation, cork taint, heat and light** (Basic): Recognise oxidation, cork taint and heat and light damage.
- **5.1.6 Sulfur, yeast and bacterial faults** (Intermediate): Recognise sulfur, yeast and bacterial faults.
- **5.1.7 Faults at threshold** (Advanced): Judge when a fault is just below or above what most tasters notice.
- **5.1.8 Judging quality** (Intermediate): Describe a wine and justify a quality and readiness judgement.
- **5.1.9 Food and wine** (Basic): Pair food and wine and explain why a pairing works.

### 5.2 Final review

Review quiz and course conclusion

- **5.2.1 Final review** (Basic): Review everything in the course.
