# BoozeTutor Beer Curriculum

The course is organised in strands. Each strand groups lessons, and each lesson teaches one or more topics; every topic says what you should be able to do once you’ve finished it.

Every topic has a level. Basic covers the essentials for enjoying and talking about a drink with confidence. Intermediate adds the grounding of a keen enthusiast or someone starting out in the trade. Advanced goes deeper, into the science, the finer points of production and regions in detail. You choose a level for each drink, and its Lessons list shows the topics up to that level.

## 1. How beer is made

### 1.1 How beer is made

From grain to glass: the brewing process

- **1.1.1 Welcome** (Basic): Know what the course covers and tell the two families of beer, ale and lager, apart.
- **1.1.2 How beer is brewed** (Basic): Describe each stage of brewing, from mashing to fermentation and packaging.

### 1.2 Beer ingredients

Malt, hops, yeast, water & adjuncts

- **1.2.1 Beer ingredients** (Basic): Explain what malt, hops, yeast, water and adjuncts each bring to a beer.

### 1.3 Brewing science

Hops, yeast, water and malt in depth

- **1.3.1 Brewing science** (Advanced): Explain the chemistry of hops and water, yeast biology, and what happens in malting and mashing.

## 2. Tasting and styles

### 2.1 Structured beer tasting

Sensory evaluation and tasting methodology

- **2.1.1 Tasting beer** (Basic): Taste beer with a structured method and trace flavours back to their ingredients.

### 2.2 Ale styles

English tradition, American craft & German ales

- **2.2.1 Ale styles** (Basic): Describe the English, American and German ale traditions and how yeast defines them.

### 2.3 Belgian & continental ales

Trappist, farmhouse, lambic & Flemish sours

- **2.3.1 Belgian and continental ales** (Basic): Describe Trappist and abbey ales, farmhouse ales, lambic and Flemish sours.

### 2.4 Lager styles

Pilsner, Helles, Bock, Dunkel & Märzen

- **2.4.1 Lager styles** (Basic): Explain how lagering works and describe the main lager styles, from Pilsner to American light lager.

### 2.5 Wheat, sour & craft beers

Hefeweizen, sour ales, strong styles & modern innovation

- **2.5.1 Wheat, sour and craft beers** (Basic): Describe wheat, sour, strong, smoked and modern craft styles, and judge a beer against its style.

## 3. Beer around the world

### 3.1 Beer regions & traditions

Belgium, Germany, Czech Republic, UK, USA & beyond

- **3.1.1 Beer regions** (Basic): Describe the brewing traditions of the great beer countries, from Belgium to Japan.

### 3.2 Beer labelling

What a label must say, and how to decode it

- **3.2.1 Beer labelling** (Basic): Read a beer label: what it must include, and what its style, strength and provenance tell you.

## 4. Serving and faults

### 4.1 Serving, pairing & draught

Temperature, glassware, food pairing & draught systems

- **4.1.1 Serving and pairing** (Basic): Serve beer at the right temperature, in the right glass and from the right system, pair it with food, and serve it responsibly.

### 4.2 Off-flavours & beer faults

Identifying and preventing common defects

- **4.2.1 Off-flavours** (Basic): Recognise the common off-flavours, their causes and how to prevent them.

### 4.3 Final review

Review quiz across all beer topics

- **4.3.1 Final review** (Basic): Review everything in the course.
