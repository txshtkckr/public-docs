# BoozeTutor Spirits curriculum

The course is organised in strands. Each strand groups lessons, and each lesson teaches one or more topics; every topic says what you should be able to do once you’ve finished it.

Every topic has a level. Basic covers the essentials for enjoying and talking about a drink with confidence. Intermediate adds the grounding of a keen enthusiast or someone starting out in the trade. Advanced goes deeper, into the science, the finer points of production and regions in detail. You choose a level for each drink, and its Lessons list shows the topics up to that level.

## 1. How spirits are made

### 1.1 How spirits are made

From raw materials to bottle: the production process

- **1.1.1 Welcome** (Basic): Find your way around the spirits course and its three levels.
- **1.1.2 How spirits are made** (Basic): Outline how a spirit is made, from raw material to bottle.
- **1.1.3 Raw materials** (Basic): Name the raw materials of the main spirits.
- **1.1.4 Fermentation** (Basic): Explain fermentation and the by-products that flavour a spirit.
- **1.1.5 Pot and column stills** (Basic): Compare pot and column stills and the spirits each makes.
- **1.1.6 Ageing in wood** (Basic): Explain what a cask gives a spirit and what an age on its label means.
- **1.1.7 Maturation and finishing** (Intermediate): Explain how maturation and finishing in wood change a spirit.
- **1.1.8 What shapes a spirit’s flavour** (Basic): Explain what shapes a spirit’s flavour, from raw material to still.

### 1.2 Distillation & maturation science

Stills, congeners, oak and time

- **1.2.1 Reflux, plates and cuts** (Advanced): Explain reflux, plates and how distillers make their cuts.
- **1.2.2 Congeners and esters** (Advanced): Explain where congeners and esters come from and how a spirit is graded by them.
- **1.2.3 Maturation chemistry** (Advanced): Explain the chemistry of maturation in oak.
- **1.2.4 The compte system** (Advanced): Explain how the compte system counts a brandy’s age.

### 1.3 Spirit tasting & evaluation

Structured evaluation approaches for professional spirit tasting

- **1.3.1 Getting started** (Basic): Compare two spirits with a structured method.
- **1.3.2 Appearance** (Basic): Describe a spirit’s clarity and colour.
- **1.3.3 Aroma** (Basic): Describe a spirit’s aromas and how intense they are.
- **1.3.4 Flavour** (Basic): Describe a spirit’s flavour, alcohol and body.
- **1.3.5 Finish** (Basic): Describe a spirit’s finish.
- **1.3.6 Assessing a spirit** (Basic): Assess a spirit’s quality and give reasons.
- **1.3.7 Nosing at dilution** (Advanced): Use dilution and blind frameworks when nosing spirits.
